# sebp — Platform Overview

**A single place to run your startup programme, end to end.**

*Status: Draft · Last updated: 2026-08-20*

---

## What sebp is

sebp is the online home of your programme. Startups discover it, apply through it,
and stay in it — and your team runs the whole selection process from the same place.

There is no separate application form, no spreadsheet tracking who is at which
stage, and no email thread where documents get lost. One platform, one record of
every applicant, one place your team works.

## The idea behind it

Most application platforms are built around one fixed process. If you change how
you select startups — add an interview round, ask for a new document, drop a step
that was not working — you file a request with a developer and wait.

sebp is built the other way around. **Your programme's process is something you
configure, not something we hard-code.** The stages, the questions asked at each
stage, and the documents required at each stage are all set up by your team through
an admin screen, and can be changed whenever you like.

This means:

- You can run this year's programme differently from last year's, without a rebuild.
- You can add a stage mid-programme if you realise you need one.
- You can run a different process for different types of applicants.
- When the programme evolves, the platform follows the same week — not the next quarter.

## For startups applying

**Sign up and create a profile.** A founder registers and sets up their startup's
profile. Co-founders can be invited to the same startup so the whole team can
contribute to the application.

**Complete the application.** The form shows exactly the questions your team has
configured — no more, no less. Progress saves as they go, so a long application can
be completed across several sittings.

**Upload required documents.** Each stage lists precisely which documents are
needed. Applicants see what is still outstanding at a glance, and are told clearly
if something is rejected and needs replacing.

**Always know where they stand.** A clear view of the current stage, what is
complete, what is still needed, and what happens next. This alone removes a large
share of the "any update?" emails a programme receives.

**Stay informed.** Notifications when a stage moves forward, a document needs
attention, an event is coming up, or an announcement is posted.

**Attend programme events.** Browse and register for sessions, workshops, and
deadlines, with reminders beforehand.

**Get help.** Raise a support request from inside the platform and see the reply in
the same place, rather than trying to remember which address they emailed.

## For your programme team

**Design the process.** Create the stages your programme runs, in the order you
want them, and decide what happens at each one. Add, rename, reorder, or retire
stages without a developer.

**Decide what to ask.** Build the questions for each stage — short text, long text,
dates, dropdowns, numbers, file uploads, and more. Mark them required or optional.
Ask the right questions at the right moment instead of one enormous form at the start.

**Set the document requirements.** Specify which documents each stage requires,
which file types are acceptable, size limits, and whether a document expires and
must be renewed.

**Review and advance applicants.** See every application, filter by stage or status,
review submitted documents, approve or reject them with a note, and move applicants
forward — or reject, or send back for changes.

**Never lose the paper trail.** Every decision, every document approval, and every
configuration change is recorded with who did it and when. If a decision is ever
questioned, the answer is on file.

**See where everyone is.** Understand how many applicants sit at each stage, where
the process is slowing down, and which reviews are overdue.

## The supporting features

### 📥 Applications and stages
The core. A configurable pipeline that applicants move along, with each stage
defining its own questions and document requirements. Applicants can be advanced,
rejected, withdrawn, or sent back for corrections.

### 📄 Document management
Secure upload and storage of required documents. Every version is kept, so a
rejected document and its replacement both remain on record. Documents can be set
to expire and be requested again. Only your team and the applicant who owns them
can access them.

### 🔔 In-app notifications
A notification centre inside the platform showing everything that has happened on
an applicant's file — stage changes, document decisions, announcements, and event
reminders.

### 📧 Email and SMS notifications
The same updates delivered to inboxes and phones, so applicants do not have to be
logged in to hear important news. Your team controls which events send messages, on
which channel, and what each message says — all editable without a developer.

### 📢 Announcements
Post updates to all applicants or to a specific group — everyone at the interview
stage, for example. Announcements can be scheduled in advance, pinned to the top,
and set to expire.

### 📅 Events
Publish programme sessions, workshops, office hours, and deadlines. Optionally
require registration, cap attendance with a waitlist, and send automatic reminders
beforehand. Attendance is recorded, and events sync to applicants' own calendars.

### 💬 Support desk
Applicants raise questions from inside the platform. Your team replies in a
threaded conversation, with private internal notes that applicants never see.
Response times are tracked.

### 🔐 Roles and permissions
Not everyone on your team needs the same access. Reviewers can assess applications;
administrators can change the programme's configuration; support staff can handle
tickets. You decide who can do what.

## What "nothing is hardcoded" means in practice

| You want to… | Traditional platform | sebp |
|---|---|---|
| Add an interview stage | Developer request, wait for a release | Admin screen, takes minutes |
| Ask a new question on the application | Developer request | Admin screen |
| Require an extra document this year | Developer request | Admin screen |
| Change the wording of a decision email | Developer request | Admin screen |
| Text applicants as well as emailing them | Developer request | Toggle a channel |
| Reorder your selection stages | Significant rework | Drag to reorder |

The trade-off worth stating honestly: building a platform this configurable takes
more work upfront than one that hard-codes a single process. The return comes from
the second year onward, and from every mid-programme change that would otherwise
have been impossible.

## Suggested delivery phases

Each phase produces something you can see and use.

**Phase 1 — Foundations.** Accounts, startup profiles, team access, and the
permission system. Your team can log in with the right level of access.

**Phase 2 — Programme configuration.** The admin screens for building stages,
questions, and document requirements. You can set up your entire programme process
before a single applicant arrives.

**Phase 3 — Applications.** Startups sign up, complete their configured
application, and your team reviews and advances them. This is the point at which
the platform can run a real programme.

**Phase 4 — Documents.** Full document upload, review, approval, versioning, and
expiry.

**Phase 5 — Notifications.** In-app, email, and SMS, with editable message
templates and control over what sends when.

**Phase 6 — Programme life.** Announcements, events with registration and
reminders, and the support desk.

**Phase 7 — Insight and polish.** Dashboards, reporting on your selection funnel,
data exports, and performance work ahead of a large intake.

An early phase order note: the configuration screens (Phase 2) come before the
application experience (Phase 3). It feels backwards, because Phase 2 produces
nothing an applicant sees. It is the right order — building the applicant
experience first would bake in a fixed process, which is the one thing this
platform exists to avoid.

## Questions we would like your input on

These shape the build, and earlier answers mean less rework later:

1. **Do several co-founders share one application, or does each startup apply
   through a single person?**
2. **Does the programme run in rounds** (a spring intake, an autumn intake) with
   different processes and separate applicant groups?
3. **What happens after a startup is accepted?** Do they keep using the platform
   through the programme itself, or does the platform's job end at selection?
4. **How do reviewers evaluate applicants** — written notes, or scores against set
   criteria with several reviewers whose scores combine?
5. **How long should we keep the documents** of applicants who are not accepted?
6. **Will the platform ever need to run in more than one language?**
